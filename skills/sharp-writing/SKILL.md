# Sharp Writing

Sharpen the author's actual thought. Work from their draft or substantive notes; if neither exists, ask for rough wording. Do not manufacture a position or ghostwrite by default.

## Find the point

Use available context to establish the intended reader, the underlying observation, the author's stance, and the desired effect. Determine any length or placement constraints. Ask only about gaps that would change the edit; use relevant supplied or accessible material first.

Look for the tension already present: an assumption worth questioning, a contradiction, an uncomfortable implication, or something left unsaid. A quiet observation may already have enough force. Do not make every draft combative, mysterious, or cynical.

If the premise is weak, unsupported, or unclear, identify that before polishing the delivery. Do not substitute your own opinion because it makes a stronger line.

## Concentrate the language

- Keep the strongest thought and remove setup that the intended reader does not need. Avoid explaining the line again after it lands.
- Preserve distinctive wording, humor, cadence, and purposeful roughness. Fragments, abrupt endings, rhetorical questions, and silence can work when they serve the thought.
- Use implication or ambiguity deliberately. The reader should have enough to grasp the tension even if its resolution remains open.
- Distinguish intriguing omission from misleading omission. Do not cut a qualification that changes the truth, certainty, scope, or target of a claim.
- Do not fabricate controversy, consensus, opponents, motives, stakes, or facts. Do not turn a qualified observation into a sweeping accusation for impact.
- Avoid stock hooks, manufactured urgency, empty contrarianism, and engagement prompts appended by habit. Provocation should come from the idea.
- A few extra words may make a short line sharper. Do not chase the smallest possible word count at the cost of meaning or voice.

Account for accompanying visuals or quoted material when relevant: what can remain unstated depends on what the reader can actually see. Ask for missing context if it determines whether the line makes sense. Follow the user's constraints without assuming a platform or fixed length.

## Deliver the refinement

Default to a brief diagnosis and one recommended version, keeping the explanation proportionate to the text. Respect critique-only requests. Offer an alternative only for a meaningful choice of tone or degree of explicitness; do not automatically supply a list of hooks.

Keep a draft unchanged when it already lands. Before delivering, check that the result preserves the author's position, stands up to a literal reading, and leaves out explanation without distorting the claim. Verify any explicit length limit. Flag a consequential change in tone or implication instead of silently making the author more extreme.
